## Order and dependencies
- 1 → 2 → 3 → 4 → 5 is the re-check chain: nothing here writes to production. It comes first because the Accenture error shows the lists cannot be trusted, and because 12 needs the "switch off" list.
- 6, 7, 8 and 9 do not depend on the re-check and could run beside it; they are ordered after it only so the owner reviews one list at a time. 6 is cheapest (config only), then 7 (may need an adapter change), then 8 (may end in a parked row), then 9 (the longest).
- 10 needs 5, 6, 7, 8 and 9. 11 needs 10. 12 needs the owner's acceptance of 11.
- Every source added needs its terms line first (criterion 1): inside slices 6-9, before the seed entry.

## Rollback
- 1: `measure_reads.ps1 -Clean` drops the scratch database.
- 2, 6, 7, 9, 10: git (nothing is committed until the owner agrees); `sitemapjobs.py` change reverted with its test.
- 3, 5, 8: evidence and `data/` files only; the `data/` lists are gitignored, so slice 5 keeps a dated copy of each list before editing it (`data/archive/`).
- 12: new sources are created disabled and can be disabled or deleted by name; the wrong sources are switched off, not deleted, so they can be switched back on; worst case, restore the Neon snapshot taken in 12(a).

## Risks
- The scratch copy dates from 29 Oct: sources added to the hosted database since then (by the owner) are not in it. Slice 12 compares names against the hosted database during the dry run.
- A stored-job check cannot tell a parent company's board from the right one when the names match: the owner reviews the `suspect` list (slice 4).
- Accenture's sitemap may list closed jobs; slice 7 checks a few pages against it.
- The 18 may take longer than the three; if a batch grows, it moves to a follow-up and this cycle closes with what is verified.
- Real-site reads: kept small and stated; robots.txt obeyed by the adapters; anything larger is asked first.
